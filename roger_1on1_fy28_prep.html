🎯 The Role, Correctly Framed

I've been thinking about how VER is positioned going into the offsite — specifically the sub-PPM designation — and I want to flag something before I walk into that room.

VER isn't a sub-function of portfolio management. It's a different function entirely. PPMs own engagements — they build timelines, manage delivery, and are accountable for spend. VER doesn't own any of that. Engagements have to be on the PPM roadmap before they even get to us. We're downstream of the plan and upstream of execution — the intelligence layer that makes decisions smarter before they're locked in.

The way I'd name it accurately:

Cross-Pillar Vendor Intelligence & Advisory Lead.
In the room at every level. Full visibility across all pillars. Accountable to the quality of intelligence — not to delivery outcomes.

This isn't about the label. It's about the data model. If VER work gets structured as portfolio engagements in Blueprint, it creates false signals — timelines with no owners, capacity that doesn't map to real execution. That's a planning integrity issue, not just a naming one.

🏛️ Why I Belong at the Offsite

I need to be in those sessions — but for a different reason than the PPMs in the room. They're there to build their pillar plans. I'm there because without cross-pillar intelligence, every pillar plan is missing a layer.

  • Renewal horizon visibility — I see multi-year vendor commitments across all pillars simultaneously. FY28 decisions need that picture early.
  • FinOps intelligence — TCO models, make-vs-buy signals, cost pressure points. None of this lives in a single pillar.
  • Vendor risk posture — governance flags, concentration risk, reliability signals. Horizontal by nature.
  • Cross-pillar pattern recognition — I see overlap, redundancy, and opportunity that no single PPM has visibility into.
"Not to build a portfolio plan — but to calibrate the intelligence that makes your plans work."
📐 FY28 — The VER Intelligence Roadmap

VER's FY28 story isn't about engagements or timelines. It's about scaling the intelligence infrastructure that already exists and formalizing how it serves the pillars.

  • Renewal horizon intelligence — proactive multi-year modeling for all major vendor contracts coming due in FY28. No surprises at renewal time.
  • TCO at intake — total cost of ownership analysis available before any new engagement is scoped. The tool exists; FY28 is about adoption across all pillars.
  • SOW intelligence — role-level cost validation for statement of work engagements before they hit the PPM roadmap. Upstream of planning.
  • Governance cadence — quarterly governance queue review across all active vendor relationships. Risk surfaced early, not reactively.
  • Stakeholder intelligence delivery — structured intake from ALL pillars, not just reactive asks. VER becomes a standing intelligence resource, not an ad hoc one.

The tooling infrastructure is built. FY28 is the year it scales from proof of concept to org-wide practice. The TCO Calculator and SOW Estimator are already in front of leadership — FY28 formalizes the intake, the cadence, and the cross-pillar reach.

⚡ Where This Connects — AI Strategy & Ops

The Cross-Pillar Intelligence Lead framing isn't just a better title for what I do today. It's the foundation for what comes next.

What VER built — organically, not by design from the top — is a methodology: AI-enabled intelligence that turns vendor complexity into clear, decision-ready outputs. TCO modeling, SOW analysis, governance intelligence, FinOps posture. All of it built and proven inside DA&O first.

The AI Strategy & Ops proposal is the formalization of that methodology. Not just for VER, not just for DA&O — but as a scalable operating model for how any function inside DET approaches AI-enabled decision intelligence.

The arc: VER proved the methodology works. Cross-Pillar Intelligence Lead is the role that bridges it. AI Strategy & Ops is the function that scales it.

The proof of concept is already running. FY28 is the formalization.

When Roger made the case at Gartner — "I can make the case for you to do this" — this is what that case looks like fully threaded. VER didn't just build tools. It built the blueprint for how DA&O operates with AI at the center of it.
