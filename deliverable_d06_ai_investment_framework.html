DA&O's framework for evaluating AI investments before they're approved — four decision gates, three spend tiers, and one rule: no AI investment moves forward without a named owner and a value case on file.
📍 The problem it solves

Right now, AI investments in DA&O get approved the way most technology decisions do — through a combination of enthusiasm, urgency, and whoever has budget authority that week. There's no standard gate. No criteria. No way to compare "should we build this?" against "do we already pay for something that does this?" The result is duplicated spend, abandoned tools, and no way to answer the basic question: was it worth it?

The Investment Decision Framework puts a structure around that conversation — without making it bureaucratic. Four questions. Three spend tiers. Clear authority at each level. Every approved investment has a value case and a named owner before a single dollar is spent.

🚦 The four decision gates

Every proposed AI investment — build, buy, or configure — passes through these four gates in order. Any gate that fails sends the initiative back for refinement, not rejection. The framework is designed to sharpen ideas, not kill them.

G1
Is there a measurable use case?
Can we define what "working" looks like in 90 days? What changes, who benefits, and how do we know it worked? A use case without a success definition is a project, not an investment.
↗ Check D01 for similar use cases first
G2
Build, buy, or configure — and why?
Has this been solved in the market? Are we already paying for a tool that covers it? The answer to those questions before a budget conversation — not after — is the Build/Buy/Configure decision framework from D04.
↗ Check D09 registry before choosing↗ Apply D04 Component 02
G3
What's the minimum value case for 90 days?
Not a three-year projection — a 90-day proof point. What does this need to deliver by the first quarterly review to justify continued investment? Time saved, cost avoided, or quality improved — with a number attached.
↗ Use D02 ROI methodology to quantify
G4
Who owns it — and owns the outcome?
One named person. Not "the team," not "AI Strategy & Ops." A specific individual who is accountable for the build, the quality bar, and the quarterly value readout. No owner, no approval.
↗ Owner name goes in D01 + D09 entries
All 4 pass
Investment enters the backlog with a scoped build plan and 90-day value target. Owner confirmed. Registry entry created.
Any gate fails
Returns to the proposer with the specific gap identified. Not a rejection — a sharpen. Re-submit when ready.
💰 Budget envelope governance

Not every AI investment needs the same level of review. The envelope tiers set clear approval authority based on cost — so teams can move fast on small bets while larger commitments get the scrutiny they deserve.

🔗 How it connects to the other deliverables

The Investment Decision Framework is the front door for the entire operating model. Every new use case that enters D01 passed through G1–G4. Every vendor or tool that enters D09 cleared G2. And D04 (the Operating Model) is what gives this framework its authority — it's not a checklist someone ignores, it's the governance layer that Roger and leadership have explicitly endorsed.
