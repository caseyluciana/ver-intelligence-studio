Direct answers to the AOC roundtable questions — what's changed, what's needed, and what we've figured out.
"What new way of working has taken root in your org that you never want to go back from?"
🔄 Proactive over reactive — every time

We don't wait for a scope to land before we start thinking about cost. We model it before the conversation starts. The TCO Calculator made that real — total cost of ownership analysis that used to require building a spreadsheet from scratch on every engagement is now a structured, scenario-ready tool available on demand. Make-vs-buy inputs, multi-year modeling, exec-ready summary. Ready before the vendor sends a quote.

Going back to reactive cost validation — analyzing after the scope is already on the table — is not something I'm willing to do. The pace changed the expectation, and the expectation is now the standard.

"What do your business partners still not understand about what operating at this pace actually requires from them?"
⚠️ Speed on our end requires specificity on theirs

They see the fast turnaround and assume the work is effortless. It's not — it's infrastructure. Every fast output is backed by a structured tool, real data, and a clear intake. What partners don't always give us is scope with enough specificity to run it cleanly.

When someone comes in with a vague make-vs-buy question and no parameters, the TCO Calculator can still run — but the output is only as good as what went in. The ask for partners is simple: come in with specificity. The sharper the input, the sharper the intelligence they get back.

"What clarity, enablement, or decision from this leadership team would most unlock your pillar's progress right now?"
🔓 Alignment that turns parallel progress into shared momentum

I don't think we're waiting on someone to figure this out — I think we're all figuring it out in parallel. What would accelerate everyone is alignment on what we're building toward together, so the best of what each team has figured out doesn't stay siloed.

The TCO Calculator has already been in front of senior leadership and it landed. That's not a VER win — that's a proof point for what's possible across the org. The thing that takes it further isn't more building in isolation. It's a shared direction from the top that lets all of us move faster, together.

"In a world where AI agents build and ship at scale, how are you measuring an employee's value differently today than a year ago?"
📐 Judgment over task execution

A year ago, value lived in the doing — pulling the data, building the model, running the numbers. The tools do that now. Value today is in the knowing.

What scenario to run. What the output actually means in context. How to frame a multi-year cost comparison so a senior leader can act on it in sixty seconds. Interpretation, judgment, and executive-ready framing — that's the new bar. The people who've grown into that mode are operating at a completely different level than they were twelve months ago.

"What is one thing your pillar has figured out that everyone else in this room should know about?"
💡 Build the proof before you make the case

Don't write a proposal for a methodology — build it, use it, and let the outputs speak. The TCO Calculator isn't a pitch for what we could do. It's evidence of what we already did. That's what makes it land differently in a room — it's not a vision, it's a live tool with a track record.

The best tools I've built weren't on anyone's roadmap. They existed because the need was real and someone had to move first. If your team is sitting on a capability that hasn't been formalized yet, the answer isn't a better slide. It's a working tool in front of a decision-maker. Ship the proof. The case makes itself.
