📐 The Position

VER is a unique function within DET — there is no other function structured to deliver cross-pillar intelligence across the full portfolio at this scope. It doesn’t fit the PPM model because it was never designed to. It operates on a different axis, with a different accountability model, its own tracking methodology, and a mandate that spans every pillar simultaneously.

Portfolio Management and Cross-Pillar Intelligence run on perpendicular axes — not a hierarchy. PPMs are vertical: one pillar, top to bottom, accountable to delivery outcomes. Cross-Pillar Intelligence is horizontal: one advisory function across every pillar simultaneously, accountable to the quality of its guidance. Cross-Pillar Intelligence doesn’t own outcomes, domains, or mandates — it advises, guides, and supports. These are two distinct functions. One cannot be a sub-function of the other because they don’t operate on the same axis.

🧩 Why VER Is Cross-Pillar Intelligence

Every characteristic of Cross-Pillar Intelligence maps to how VER has operated. The scope isn’t limited to DA&O — VER advises and supports pillars across all of DET. That’s not a sub-PPM footprint. That’s a cross-pillar advisory function.

  • VER serves pillars across DET, not one pillar, not one org. TCO analysis, renewal modeling, vendor risk, SOW intelligence — available to any pillar that reaches for it. Sub-PPM would mean one column of one sub-org. That has never been the operating model.
  • VER’s work is advisory intelligence, not delivery output. No timelines, no milestones, no engagements on a roadmap. VER is activated by a question — a contract coming up, a cost to pressure-test, a vendor relationship showing risk — and responds with analysis and guidance. The stakeholder acts; VER advises.
  • The tooling VER built reflects the function’s shape. The TCO Calculator, SOW Estimator, renewal horizon modeling, governance support — every tool was designed to serve the full portfolio, not a single program. You don’t build horizontal tools if you’re a vertical function.
  • Structuring VER as sub-PPM would immediately collapse its scope. A sub-PPM reports into one pillar. The moment Cross-Pillar Intelligence reports into one pillar, it’s no longer cross-pillar — and the intelligence that depends on seeing across all pillars simultaneously disappears with it.
Cross-Pillar Intelligence across DET is the accurate description of what VER already does. The name reflects the function — not the other way around.
✨ Why This Function Matters

There is a category of intelligence no single PPM can produce — patterns that only emerge when you look across every pillar simultaneously. The renewal compounding across three pillars. The vendor concentration risk invisible inside any one program. The cost pressure nobody flagged because nobody had the full picture.

Cross-Pillar Intelligence exists to make that signal available, early, without asking any PPM to carry an intelligence burden that isn’t their core job.

Without this function

Each pillar makes decisions with column-level visibility. Renewals surface at the last minute. Vendor risk emerges reactively. Cost patterns nobody caught become delivery problems.

With this function

Every pillar has access to portfolio-level signal before decisions lock. PPMs focus on delivery because the intelligence layer is already working upstream.

  • No surprises at renewal. Multi-year visibility across every vendor relationship — available before the window closes.
  • Cost clarity before commitment. TCO and SOW analysis available upstream of any scoping decision — so cost pressure is understood before it locks in.
  • Risk surfaced early, not reactively. Vendor concentration, governance flags, reliability signals — visible across the full portfolio before they become delivery noise.
🔑 How It Works

There is one area where VER is a required touchpoint: third-party spend. Any approved initiative that involves vendor engagement, SOW execution, or external spend moves through VER before it executes — a required checkpoint that ensures the cost model, vendor risk, and governance posture are understood before commitment.

The key distinction: the initiative lives on the PPM roadmap. PPMs and sub-pillar PPMs own it as their work item — they drive it, they deliver it. VER’s role activates specifically on the third-party spend component of that initiative. VER doesn’t carry the work item. It advises on the vendor dimension of work that’s already been approved and scoped by the PPM.

VER’s work is tracked through its own intelligence methodology — not as PPM roadmap items. Advisory requests, vendor assessments, SOW analyses, renewal models, governance reviews — these are the units of VER’s work. Forcing them into Blueprint as portfolio engagements creates false signals: timelines with no delivery owners, capacity that doesn’t map to real execution. The tracking system has to match the function.

Neither function reports into the other. Neither replaces the other. Portfolio Management executes and tracks delivery. Cross-Pillar Intelligence advises on vendor and spend intelligence, tracked through its own methodology. The org needs both — and needs them structured correctly to work.

One advisory function. Every pillar. Present before the decision is made, available whenever the question exists. Not a sub-function of portfolio management — a different kind of function entirely.
